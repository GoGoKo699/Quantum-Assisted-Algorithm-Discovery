## The objective

Use circuit-model quantum computation to obtain information that improves a
scientifically or practically useful computation against strong classical methods.
The project originally emphasized discovery of reusable classical algorithms.
The owner subsequently permitted direct quantum samples and asked for model-first
analysis. Those extensions remain in force; quantum-free deployment is optional.

Do not replace usefulness with an artificial algebra problem, a large Hilbert
space, a conveniently inaccurate classical baseline, or unnecessary precision.
A conditional mechanism can merit investigation without a universal classical
lower bound, a new primitive, a dataset, or a hardware demonstration.

## Starting the next exploration

No candidate or new scientific phase is created by this maintenance pass.
On the next explicit research continuation, read the current work order and
Revision 27. Compare a few short **useful model–mechanism pairs** before selecting
one. A candidate statement must join the following in a single argument:

1. The independently useful question, established model, observable, and needed accuracy.
2. The concrete quantum operation and its preparation, access, time, readout, and reuse costs.
3. The strongest adequate classical acquisition or direct-output route.
4. One structural reason the quantum route could improve the useful computation.
5. One bounded calculation capable of rejecting that reason.

Do not automatically enlarge the seven-emitter system, tighten epsilon, make the
detector finer, or return to generic error bounds. Reopening a closed lead needs
new evidence for a useful regime and a specific bottleneck; a larger formal state
space is not new evidence. The quantum operation and its useful consequence must
be part of the same proposal, not connected after an elaborate construction.

Land the note, applicable code/report, provenance, and current decision before
moving to another candidate. New work belongs in a versioned successor; preserve
existing scientific bytes and negative findings. Do not merge branches, release,
revive manuscripts, contact others, or initiate paid/unattended work by inference.
