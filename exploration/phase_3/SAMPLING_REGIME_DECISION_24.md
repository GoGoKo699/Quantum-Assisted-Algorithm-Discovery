Branch: `research/prx-quantum-phase2`. Manuscript remains on hold.

**Decision:** the one-dimensional spectral construction remains a quantitative
reference, not an established quantum-advantage workload. The existing bounds do
not identify a regime that is both demonstrably demanding for adequate classical
methods and cheaper quantumly. This is NOT a proof that the chain is easy at all
resolutions. Stop adding generic spectral certificates as the default task.

Correct the comparison first: a quantum producer can also amortize sample
acquisition in a classical output table. Such reuse must not be confused with
fresh statistical information. Then examine one distinct direct-output hypothesis:
physical emission records of a driven, dissipative spin system. A record is a
history generated by quantum measurements, not a classical trajectory filtered by
Grover search or a spectrum reconstructed from a few scalar correlations.
No quantum advantage, new simulation primitive, or publication novelty is claimed.
No new repository is needed. Earlier notes and their conditional theorems stand.

Every competitor receives the same model and required accuracy. The classical

- random-phase typicality with full-time pure-state propagation (Note 23);

## 2. Quantum-produced tables: ordinary learning is available to both sides

Let q be a quantum producer's distribution on B finite bin labels, with
TV(p,q)<=eta relative to the required p. Draw S independent original samples and
let qhat be their empirical distribution. Standard finite-distribution learning
[1] gives an explicit sufficient estimate. One may use

$$
S\geq\left\lceil
\frac{(\sqrt{B-1}+\sqrt{2\log(1/\beta)})^2}{4\epsilon_{\rm bank}^2}
\right\rceil.
$$

Then TV(p,qhat)<=eta+epsilon_bank with probability at least 1-beta. Derivation:
E TV(q,qhat)<=sqrt((B-1)/S)/2 by binomial variances and Cauchy-Schwarz; changing
one sample changes TV by at most 1/S, so one-sided bounded differences gives the
remaining term. This is standard empirical learning, not a new theorem.
Dependence on quantum shot correlations, drift or adaptive acquisition would
need a different justified concentration statement.

For the prior B=10002 bins, epsilon_bank=0.02 and beta=0.01, the displayed
worst-case sufficient budget is 6,635,758 original draws. This is not a minimum,
a proposal to run that many shots, or a reason to change the requested bins.
A smooth-spectrum representation could improve the upper bound but must itself
be justified. Only the consequence for the comparison is needed here:

$$
C_Q(M)\leq\min\{M q_Q,\ S q_Q+C_{\rm table}+M c_{\rm table}\}.
$$

The classical side may use its best table, direct draw or learned sampler too.
More total draws cannot be assumed to make the direct-quantum family inevitably
lose merely because ONE implementation repeats the device call. Conversely, a
bank does not make acquiring accurate original information free. In a comparison
between fixed acceptable tables the common M-dependent draw cost may cancel;
the acquisition costs still decide the result. A crossover between two upper
bounds is not a comparison with the best possible classical algorithm.

### Reuse is not fresh information

For an event of probability p, take S independent original draws, then make M
independent draws from their empirical table. Conditional on that table they are
independent; unconditionally they share its error. Their event-frequency variance is

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\bar Y)=p(1-p)\left[\frac1S+
\frac{1-1/S}{M}\right]
=p(1-p)\frac{S+M-1}{SM}.
$$

This follows by conditioning on the original empirical frequency. For this mean,
the equivalent number of fresh independent draws is SM/(S+M-1), at most S.
Thus 10,000 original draws replayed a million times provide about 9,901 effective
draws for this statistic, not a million. This is a statistic-specific variance
equivalence, not a universal definition of effective sample size.

The guarantee for a fixed approximate law and the inference guarantee from M
fresh target draws are different contracts. For joint-M-output TV one safe bound
is beta+M(eta+epsilon_bank), capped at one; meeting a fixed joint tolerance may
require increasingly accurate acquisition. The same issue applies to a noisy
classical trace table. No amount of resampling removes either table's uncertainty.

## 3. What we can and cannot conclude

The chain has an explicit quantum sampler and several nontrivial classical
competitors. The arguments do not yet isolate a necessary response component
that those competitors cannot obtain adequately and cheaply. Entanglement of a
full vector, a large sufficient window and increasing nominal n are insufficient.
The fair conclusion is **no demonstrated advantage regime**, not **no possible
advantage**. Keep the spectral work as a reusable comparison, without converting
its classical bounds into a third spin-off or spending indefinitely on certificates.

The next mechanism test should change the mathematical information being sampled,
not just choose a harder-looking molecule. We select the following bounded check.

## 4. Additional direct-output hypothesis: monitored emission histories

An independently motivated consumer is temporal photon-emission statistics:
intermittency, waiting times, and correlations between bright and dark periods
in driven dissipative interacting emitters. Ates et al. [2] explicitly study such
records for a dissipative transverse-field Ising model. Their Markovian model,
local decay, activity statistics and Rydberg realization discussion supply the
application anchor. Malossi et al. [3] independently measured counting distributions
in a dissipative Rydberg gas; those are excitation-number measurements, NOT the
site-resolved photon-time records proposed here. We do not claim to reproduce
that experiment or infer the new record law from its histogram.

A first model is the published local-spin family (S=Pauli/2):

$$
H=\Omega\sum_iS_i^x+V\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}S_i^zS_j^z,
\quad J_i=\sqrt{\kappa}\,S_i^-,\quad
\dot\rho=-i[H,\rho]+\sum_i(J_i\rho J_i^\dagger-
\tfrac12\{J_i^\dagger J_i,\rho\}).
$$

Use a specified finite bounded-degree lattice, parameters, product initial state,
horizon T and detector time bins. A zero-temperature Markovian reservoir is the
model convention; this is NOT the previous infinite-temperature closed-spin trace.
Nearest-neighbor truncation, independent decay channels and ideal site-resolved
unit-efficiency photon counting are stated assumptions, not established hardware
properties of all Rydberg experiments. Detector inefficiency, unresolved sites,
dephasing and finite timing must be included when the chosen consumer needs them.
No unknown stationary or thermal state is supplied free: a stationary record task
would add burn-in/mixing costs. We start from the declared product state.

For a pure starting state, a record r={(t_1,i_1),...,(t_m,i_m)} has density

$$
P(r)=\|e^{-iH_{\rm eff}(T-t_m)}J_{i_m}
 e^{-iH_{\rm eff}(t_m-t_{m-1})}\cdots
 J_{i_1}e^{-iH_{\rm eff}t_1}\psi_0\|^2,
\quad H_{\rm eff}=H-\tfrac i2\sum_iJ_i^\dagger J_i,
$$

with the chronological no-jump propagators between every pair of jumps understood.
The density is with respect to ordered event times and the discrete detector
labels; it includes the zero-event outcome and sums/integrates to one. This is
the standard quantum-jump construction [4], not a new stochastic primitive.
The digital task is its stated coarse-grained record law, with overflow handled
if a finite output representation caps the event count.

Quantum integration is direct: local unitary evolution plus monitored
system-ancilla interactions can generate successive output bits while retaining
the conditional system state on qubits. No spectral reconstruction, classical
trajectory-rejection oracle or rare postselection is required for ordinary records.
A concrete finite-step amplitude-damping instrument has

$$
A_0=|g\rangle\langle g|+e^{-\kappa h/2}|e\rangle\langle e|,
\quad A_1=\sqrt{1-e^{-\kappa h}}|g\rangle\langle e|.
$$

One initialized ancilla and a controlled rotation, followed by measurement and
reset, implement it. Interleaving with local Hamiltonian steps is an exact
positive *discrete collision model*. Its approximation to the continuous driven
record law is a SEPARATE error to bound, including multiple events per time bin.
We have not proved a compiled whole-record complexity bound for that limit here.
Locality gives polynomial gate work per finite step; it does not make the number
of required steps, output length or detector model free.

### Specify the instrument, not only the master equation

Let I_j be a channel whose output contains the new classical record and remaining
quantum state. If each approximate instrument has half-diamond error at most
eta_j, contraction and telescoping give full-record TV <=sum_j eta_j, plus any
initial-state error. A bound on only the channel after erasing the record does
NOT supply this guarantee. This is an elementary channel-composition argument.

An exact two-emitter control makes the distinction concrete. For independent
amplitude damping with p=1/4 and initial (|eg>+|ge>)/sqrt(2), local detector labels
have probabilities (no,1,2,double)=(3/4,1/8,1/8,0). Mix the single-emission Kraus
operators into their symmetric/antisymmetric combinations: the unlabelled channel
is unchanged but the labelled probabilities become (3/4,1/4,0,0). Their TV is 1/8.
Coarsening to the total count erases the difference. This is not a computational
hardness example; it prevents us from claiming a record guarantee from the wrong
mathematical object. The proposed detector cannot be changed to ease simulation.

## 5. Classical shortcuts remain legitimate in the new model

The classical quantum-jump method [4] already evolves one conditional pure state,
not an entire density matrix. Mean-field, cluster, tensor and hidden-state models
must be allowed at the requested record quality. Independent emitters admit small
classical renewal descriptions; even quantum antibunching is not by itself a
many-body computational advantage. Symmetric collective models and few-excitation
sectors may be polynomially small too.

Strong dephasing in related driven Rydberg models yields an established classical
rate equation with kinetic constraints [5]. Slow relaxation, intermittency and
many possible histories do not themselves certify a quantum obstruction. The new
candidate must identify useful temporal information not reproduced adequately by
a justified classical reduction; a large record alphabet alone proves nothing.
If the consumer needs only the mean count or a few cumulants, permit direct
classical evaluation of those rather than insist on sampling every history.
Caching and the information limit above continue to apply.

**Next bounded task:** derive a record-level quantum discretization/error budget
and compare it with a physically justified classical rate/renewal reduction in
one coherent-interaction regime. Choose a fixed finite-time statistic of dark
intervals or consecutive count bins that the application uses. Track detector
coarse-graining and conditional-state information, not just density-matrix error
or entanglement. A small calculation may check the law; it must not be presented
as a hard or useful new device. No large trajectory campaign, hardware port,
new data dependency or generic quantum-trajectory framework is authorized.
The spectral/chaotic-dynamics mechanisms remain available; this is not a universal
rejection of them or a claim that the new lead already succeeds.
