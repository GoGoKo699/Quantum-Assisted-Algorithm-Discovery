# Strategic revision 27: choose a quantum opportunity, not the next available lemma

29 September 2026. Reviewed head: `4d6204640d70d006ae637ce4c7830a033832a8d5`.
Active branch: `research/prx-quantum-phase2`. Manuscript preparation stays on hold.

**Decision:** preserve the existing mathematics, stop automatic development of
more general-purpose certificates, and retain monitored emitters only as a
candidate for one bounded regime-selection test. The next deliverable must make
the useful observable, quantum mechanism, and strongest adequate classical
comparison part of the SAME claim. No useful quantum advantage or publication
centerpiece is established. No new repository or third classical spin-off is needed.

This is a strategic and source-applicability revision, not a complete independent
proof audit, a new theorem, or an experimental checkpoint.

## 1. Preserve the owner's actual objective

The original parent sought useful classical methods discovered quantumly. The
owner subsequently authorized direct quantum samples and model-first exploration.
Those are deliberate changes, not errors to reverse. The continuing objective is
information that makes a consequential computation more effective using quantum
processing. A learned classical program and quantum-free deployment are optional.
A real physical question and an honest comparison remain necessary.

Model-first means start from established equations and a motivated observable,
then identify the quantum operation and its essential input/error costs. It does
not require a dataset, native software or hardware demonstration before exploring
a mechanism. Equally, the ability to prove another error bound does not determine
which question deserves the next research round.

The prior charter explicitly requires no theorem at every checkpoint. We should
use that freedom. Auxiliary mathematics can be valuable without satisfying the
parent goal, and a conditional scientific hypothesis can merit investigation
without a universal lower bound against all classical algorithms.

## 3. What we should correct in the working method

### Correctness progress is not automatically advantage progress

The record instrument, positive spectral outputs, costed preparation, detector
coarse-graining, and error accounting prevent misleading claims. They remain
useful. But repeated refinements largely answered whether a proposed construction
is legitimate, or when an existing classical reduction works. They have not yet
supplied the central mechanism-and-benefit argument.

The new discipline is not to weaken correctness. It is to choose the next proof
because it can decide that argument. Neither an additional note nor a larger count
of passing controls is a research-value metric.

### Do not manufacture difficulty by strengthening the output contract

Full site/time-record TV and record-plus-final-state accuracy are mathematically
well-defined. They are also potentially stronger than predicting the scientifically
used activity statistic. Record-plus-state norm bounds are useful sufficient
proof devices; the physical user need not request that final quantum state.
If the consumer needs a bounded probability or a small collection of correlations,
allow direct evaluation. Do not require a microscopic history merely because it
is harder classically. Conversely, do not silently coarsen the original task and
claim to have solved its finer version. Explicitly define any narrower useful task.

### Do not make a universal classical lower bound a prerequisite

A promising regime can be supported by an explicit quantum resource improvement,
a justified limitation of the strongest applicable classical methods, and one
falsifiable unresolved condition. That is not an all-classical separation. Label
it accordingly. The absence of such a separation theorem is not a reason to stop
all theoretical exploration. A comparison only with an intentionally weak rate
model, however, is insufficient.

### Distinguish cheap representation from cheap acquisition

A two-state effective process can be inexpensive to execute while its switching
rates or within-phase emission statistics are costly to determine from the input
Hamiltonian. Charge that acquisition to both sides. Quantum assistance could in
principle act there; this revision establishes no such benefit. Equally, a supplied
small matrix, fitted rates, measured training set, or accurate stationary state
must not be granted freely to either algorithm.

### Keep provenance and scope visible

were explicitly distinguished in Note 25. The live branch governs continuation;
the attached alternative remains evidence, not an authorization to overwrite it.
The public project map is dated and the direct-output/model-first extensions live
in AGENTS.md. Earlier current/next headings are checkpoint records, not parallel
work orders. No additional branch, duplicate project or data-cleanup campaign is
needed for this revision.

## 4. A material source correction: the classical literature already reaches records

Rose et al. [1] study a periodic driven dissipative Ising chain. They identify
two metastable phases and classical long-time switching in specified regimes.
This is not our open-chain, ground-start, short-time control by default; the
boundary, parameter regime, initial transient and observation scale need matching.

More importantly, Macieszczak et al. [2] analyze coarse continuous-measurement
records, not only averaged states. Section V B 4 relates time-binned activity to
classical trajectories under classical-metastability and scale-separation
conditions; local activity is also discussed. Section V B 3 retains within-phase
fluctuation and initial-transient corrections. Thus rejecting a bare Poisson
telegraph model would not reject the strongest phase-level comparator.

These sources do not automatically certify our complete fine record in TV or
supply cheap model extraction for arbitrary size. The point is methodological:
"average dynamics do not fix detector records" remains true, but cannot stand in
for inspecting existing record-aware classical theory.

## 5. The remaining candidate, stated without a hidden answer

For the driven Ising emitter family, determine whether a physically motivated
finite-time counting observable requires conditional information beyond the
adequate classical descriptions, OR whether acquiring the otherwise compact
classical description exposes a concrete quantum-improvable bottleneck.

The natural question is the accuracy of a phase-level description of emission
fluctuations across observation timescales. This has an independent physics anchor
[1-3]; it is not a need for arbitrary precision invented by the algorithm. Do not
assume that the equal-scale few-site tests establish metastability or that a
failure of a population approximation already answers this question.

The relevant quantities are internal relaxation time, switching time, detector
bin duration, observation horizon, and the size/precision of the conditional
information needed for the selected output. A separated-timescale picture is a
hypothesis to check, not a new assumption that every parameter choice satisfies.
A few eigenvalues without control of the associated approximation are not a
complete convergence or record-error certificate.

## 6. The next deliverable and stopping rule

Produce ONE short model-specific claim sheet, not another generic simulator:

1. **Observable and regime.** Start with a finite-time emission-activity question
   already motivated by the sources. State graph/boundaries, initial state,
   physical parameters, detection/coarsening, horizon and justified absolute error.
   Do not supply an equilibrated initial state free or select finer timing solely
   to defeat a classical approximation. No numerical precision is imposed here.
2. **Strongest matched classical description.** Map the assumptions of [1,2] to
   that same problem, including transient and within-phase terms. Include the
   coherent few-excitation, tensor/cluster, event-driven conditional-state and
   direct-statistic alternatives where applicable. Price model acquisition and
   reuse as well as generation of new information.
3. **Quantum leverage and one decisive test.** Identify which costly operation
   Note 25's quantum conditional evolution avoids or improves. Specify a bounded
   derivation or modest calculation that would reject the claim, not merely verify
   the local gates. Existing conditional bounds are enough to start; no universal
   classical lower bound or hardware implementation is required.

Proceed to substantive development only when those items fit together. A
small classical description with inexpensive acquisition redirects the emitter
candidate to the reference library, not to another broad classical project.
A specific quantum opportunity with one unresolved assumption earns a focused
calculation. Failure of the present phase-level approximation alone earns neither
an advantage claim nor automatic promotion of a harder graph or finer detector.
If the claim sheet still contains only "the many-body state might be hard",
keep the evidence and broaden the mechanism search.

The immediate task is this applicability/leverage decision. We are NOT yet choosing
an exact microscopic record as the application, proving a new hidden-state theorem,
asserting a quantum transition-rate advantage, or launching larger trajectories.
