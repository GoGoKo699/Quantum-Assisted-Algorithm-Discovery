## 1. The previous early exit does not exhaust the classical options

Let G be a finite abelian group with explicit uniquely encoded operations and
L<=N=|G|<=U a rigorous interval. The divisor-only test from note22 returns N when
only one multiple is possible. If several remain, full QUANTUM relation recovery
is not automatically necessary. Classical exponent-first group algorithms and
Sylow methods already supply strong alternatives [1,2].

Now require a COMPLETE generating list D_1,...,D_k with exact certified orders
r_i, and let E=lcm(r_i). Then E is the group exponent. Its prime factorization
comes from the order certificates and is verified. Factoring and order acquisition
must be priced; the diagnostic supplies them classically. Generator completeness
is a distinct premise, probabilistic for the large-field point sampler. Exact
orders of an incomplete list do not establish completeness.

Define c=N/E and the computable upper bound C=floor(U/E). In an abelian group
there is an element of order E, so c is the index of a largest cyclic subgroup.
The new completion is polynomial in the VALUE of C, not log C. It does not make
arbitrary finite abelian groups classically easy.

## 2. Only small prime components can remain unresolved

Write E=product_l l^e_l and v_l(N)=e_l+d_l. Since c=product_l l^d_l<=C,

    l>C implies d_l=0, hence |G_l|=l^e_l.

That primary component has an element of order l^e_l and is cyclic. Its
cardinality is already known. No large-prime discrete logarithm or coordinate
decoder is required: our output is N, not an arbitrary element's coordinates.

Let E_s contain the prime powers of E with l<=C and E_b=E/E_s. Multiplication
by E_b maps G onto its small-prime part G_s, whose order is E_s*c. The input
prime support must be complete; a mere divisor of N would not justify this split.

For each small prime l, choose an original generator attaining l^e_l in its order
and form A_l=(E/l^e_l)D_i. Then A=sum_l A_l has order E_s. This is checked using
the factored order. Consequently G_s/<A> has c<=C cosets. We count those cosets,
not every element of G_s.

## 3. Exact membership in the large smooth cyclic subgroup

For a with order l^e in an ambient abelian group of exponent l^e, test x in <a>
by the ordinary prime-power digit-lifting procedure. At stage j, multiply the
current residual by l^(e-1-j) and compare with the l-entry table generated by
l^(e-1)a. If absent, reject membership; otherwise subtract the corresponding
digit times l^j a. After e digits, require zero residual.

For members this is Pohlig-Hellman. For arbitrary x the final zero residual is
also a certificate of the membership found; no membership promise is assumed.
Apply this separately to the prime-power projections of x and A. The prime sizes
are at most C, although the exponent E_s can be huge. Stronger classical methods
[2] may improve this elementary implementation and remain eligible competitors.

Enumerate cosets by a frontier search starting at identity, adding each projected
generator and testing against existing representatives. Once the frontier closes,
the number of representatives is c and N=E*c. A cap violation requests fallback;
it never asserts infeasibility or an inferred cardinality.

With B=max(1,ceil(log2 E)), at most O(k C^2) membership tests suffice. A deliberately
loose bound, including linear equality lookup instead of assumed constant-time
hashing, is

    O(k C^2 (B^2+C B) + k B^2 + C B)

primitive group/equality operations, plus polynomial integer arithmetic. The
simple implementation stores O(CB+C+k) encoded elements. Group-operation and
encoding costs multiply these counts. This is not a gate or hardware estimate.
The method needs no full relation lattice or invariant-factor decomposition.

## 4. The computable cofactor bound is not artificially loose in our regime

Use the exact integer Weil interval from note22. Its unrounded upper/lower ratio is

    ((sqrt(Q)+1)/(sqrt(Q)-1))^(2g).

For Q>=64g^2, s=sqrt(Q)>=8g, and the logarithm of the ratio is
4g atanh(1/s) <= (4g/s)/(1-1/s^2) <=32/63<log 2. Thus U<2L<=2N, so

    c <= C < 2c.

The calculated budget parameter is within a factor two of the actual largest-
cyclic-subgroup index, without using the unknown N to select an interval.
This does NOT prove that c is small on any chosen family or across all required
extension fields. For (Z/lZ)^d it is l^(d-1), and the completion is not efficient
in the bit length of that general input. Group structure may change with field
extension. The classical source may also reveal an easier answer than this method.

Asymptotic cyclicity results for selected isogeny-class ensembles [6] do not supply
an unproved distributional guarantee for our curves and their full extension
schedule. No such average-case or random-cyclicity promise is used.

## 5. The quantum part becomes narrower on the small-C branch

The conditional workflow is:

    classical complete-list construction with its error budget
    -> quantum element-order acquisition and required factorization
    -> classical certificate checks, E and C
    -> bounded classical cofactor completion if C is affordable
    -> otherwise the established complete generic backend
    -> exact classical zeta reconstruction.

The additional multi-generator quantum relation-bank stage can be omitted on
the accepted branch. Relative to note21, the initial m*c0*a controlled translations
for order-acquisition tuples remain; the later max(0,s-c0)*m*b term is unnecessary
there. That comparison is not a gate saving until the completion work, factoring,
confidence and full reversible group arithmetic are priced.

Standard order/factoring algorithms [3] and full abelian decomposition [4] are
prior work. All generic controlled translations still act on general Jacobian
elements, not just degree-one point generators. A small quotient does not give
free initial orders or a small reversible field multiplier. The fallback remains
necessary for large C, and its cost is included rather than conditioning a claimed
runtime on success. A predeclared C cap can decide the branch after order information
has been acquired. No dedicated preliminary quantum pass is required if that
information already arises in the generic backend.

The classical competitor gets the same exponent-first completion, interval bounds,
source information, factoring options, amortization and more efficient Sylow
algorithms. Quantum advantage, if any, must arise in the remaining implemented
order computation on a useful input family, not in denying this completion to
the classical side. No new PRX Quantum contribution has been established here.

## 6. Actual checks

The generic routine receives only group operations, element encodings, certified
orders and interval data, not a coordinate decoder or true cardinality.

- 792 exact completions on 88 small direct products, with three generating-list
  variants and three upper bounds. Non-basis and redundant lists are included.
- 842 accepted exact-order certificates and 842 rejected nonminimal orders.
- 221 cyclic-membership comparisons including nonmembers.
- Three visible controls Z/(101*2^e) x Z/4 x Z/2, e=20,40,80. The interval gives
  C=9. Each completion counts eight cosets with 101 membership tests and a two-
  entry digit table; the prime101 is skipped. At e=80 the group has
  976812062248620373162590208 elements, none of which are enumerated as a full
  set. The direct-product source makes the answer trivial classically. These
  are algebraic execution controls, NOT a hidden advantage workload.
- All164 inherited small curves, with complete lists and orders produced by
  independent classical enumeration of their entire small Jacobians. The
  interval-only rule settles seven; completion settles the remaining157 also.
  Max C is27 and max actual c is6. This is not a large-field success-rate study,
  nor the actual quantum generator/order acquisition workflow.
- Sixty exact arithmetic checks of the Weil-interval ratio at valid prime-power
  field sizes satisfying Q>=64g^2; no curve was constructed at each parameter.

The incomplete-list control supplies two generators of (Z/2)^3; it returns the
four-element subgroup under a wide interval, not the ambient eight-element group.
Even the correct ambient exponent does not certify a complete generating list.
The (Z/2)^10 control explicitly requests fallback when C=1023 exceeds its cap64.
A nonminimal order certificate is rejected before completion.

The new verifier reproduces its report byte-for-byte and reruns the preceding
backend verifier unchanged. No native production group-structure package, quantum
order finder, large-curve counter, elementary-gate resource estimate, hardware
experiment or root historical verifier was run. All orders in the diagnostics
are classically supplied. No spinoff or external service was changed.

## 7. Research decision

Keep this as an exact conditional simplification and a strengthened classical
baseline, not as an independent new project. It identifies a regime where long-
period discovery is the main remaining quantum role while noncyclic completion
is bounded classical work. The scientific gate remains a justified task where
those periods are hard enough classically, cheap enough quantumly, and sufficient
to produce a valuable invariant. Neither that regime nor the complete cost is
demonstrated by the controls. Manuscript stays on hold; no new repository is
requested. The wider compact-input algebraic direction remains open.
