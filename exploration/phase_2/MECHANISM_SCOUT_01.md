# Mechanism scout 01: compile a family of computations, not a list of answers

25 September 2026. Phase 2 exploration. Target: PRX Quantum. Manuscript remains on hold.

This is a literature-and-mechanism note, not a new algorithm, benchmark, novelty claim, or publication draft. No quantum or classical solver was implemented in this pass.

## 1. A positive template already exists

Apers and de Wolf [1] give a quantum algorithm producing an explicit classical spectral graph sparsifier. For an undirected weighted graph with n vertices and m edges, their theorem has running time ~O(sqrt(mn)/epsilon) and output size ~O(n/epsilon^2), for epsilon >= sqrt(n/m). The comparison with ~O(m) classical work is in the stated adjacency-list access model. At fixed epsilon and m=Theta(n^2), these are ~O(n^(3/2)) and ~O(n^2).

The paper's time model includes coherent graph queries and quantum-read/classical-write RAM of ~O(sqrt(mn)/epsilon) classical bits. Its O(log n) work-qubit statement does NOT describe a complete small circuit-model machine. The paper permits removing QRAM for query-complexity purposes at the price of extra gates; that does not by itself preserve the time speedup. Full memory access, arithmetic, construction of the input representation, and hardware implementation must be priced for this project.

This is prior work, not the contribution we seek. Its value for exploration is that it already has the right output shape: a reusable classical structure, not a quantum answer state. The quantum algorithm does not enumerate all sparsifiers and test them. It uses graph structure and quantum subroutines to construct one.

## 2. Why this output can replace a family of future computations

For a weighted graph, write its Laplacian as L. A spectral sparsifier with Laplacian L_tilde satisfies

    (1-epsilon) x^T L x <= x^T L_tilde x <= (1+epsilon) x^T L x

simultaneously for every real vector x, with high probability over construction [2]. The stored output is a smaller reweighted edge list. Once the guarantee holds, later vectors can be chosen after seeing the output; the guarantee is not limited to a training distribution. The graph/operator itself is fixed: this is not a guarantee for arbitrary later changes to its weights.

As an elementary consequence, take lambda>0 and 0<epsilon<1 and put

    A = L + lambda I,     A_tilde = L_tilde + lambda I.

Then (1-epsilon)A <= A_tilde <= (1+epsilon)A in the positive-semidefinite order. For exact solutions x=A^(-1)b and x_tilde=A_tilde^(-1)b,

    ||x_tilde-x||_A <= epsilon/(1-epsilon) ||x||_A.

Proof: B=A^(-1/2) A_tilde A^(-1/2) has spectrum in [1-epsilon,1+epsilon]. Thus ||B^(-1)-I||_2 <= epsilon/(1-epsilon); apply it to A^(-1/2)b. This derivation is routine, not a proposed new theorem. Numerical solver error is additional. Sparse matrix-vector evaluation uses the stored edges, but whether the whole solver beats a specialized classical method for the original operator remains a separate question.

The useful object is therefore a replacement operator usable on new right-hand sides, not a table of precomputed solutions. It is a reusable classical representation; it is not automatically a new general-purpose arithmetic algorithm.

## 3. The possible research question

Can quantum computation construct a useful compressed operator from a compact classical specification, without first expanding its much larger interaction list?

A possible interface is a classical specification D and a weight-generation rule w_D(i,j). The desired output is a sparse classical surrogate with a simultaneous guarantee for the required downstream computations. This is a task sketch, not a selected real workload. An independently needed operator and tolerance must be identified before it becomes an application claim.

Both competitors receive D and may inspect it. Coherent weight evaluation must be compiled from D, with its data access and precision included. Computing weights on demand does not eliminate the sparsification algorithm's internal memory-access requirements. Nor does an oracle lower bound for arbitrary graphs apply automatically to graphs generated by a short formula.

The original explicit-input requirement remains. Giving a physical quantum process or an opaque oracle as the input is a different model, which must be declared rather than substituted silently.

## 4. Classical objections that the candidate must survive

If an arbitrary m-edge file must be read and loaded into a quantum-accessible structure, its one-time input processing is already Omega(m) in the serial work model. That can remove the headline sublinear advantage. A pre-existing data structure is a different starting condition; neither its availability nor its physical access costs should be invented.

If D generates a structured dense graph, the classical competitor may also avoid expanding it. Bakshi et al. [3] give subquadratic kernel-matrix algorithms, including spectral sparsification, through density estimation and weighted sampling. Alman et al. [4] give algorithms and conditional hardness distinctions for geometric-graph primitives. Thus comparison against a naive n-by-n matrix construction is inadequate.

For fixed epsilon and m=O(n), the quoted sparsification bounds do not provide a speedup in the exponent of n. This is another reason to choose the task by its actual structure rather than by the label 'graph problem'.

Reuse is not an exclusive quantum benefit. If both methods produce equally useful structures and later executions cost E per use, a matched comparison is

    T_Q = D_Q + R E,      T_C = D_C + R E.

It is wrong to charge the classical competitor for reconstructing its sparsifier R times. More generally, compare with a different classical surrogate or a method that bypasses surrogate construction altogether.

Output correctness is supplied probabilistically by the algorithm's theorem. Independently verifying a large spectral guarantee can itself cost work; such verification is not free merely because the output is classical and small.

## 5. Two other sketches examined

### Sparse Fourier representation

A naive proposal is to find a short Fourier expansion quantumly, then use it classically. But Kushilevitz--Mansour [5] already provides polynomial-time membership-query learning for the relevant class of functions approximable by polynomially sparse Fourier expansions, under its norm/distribution guarantees. The explicit classical evaluator can supply membership queries too. This blocks a generic exponential-advantage story, not every possible polynomial improvement or every spectral-learning task.

### A classical simulator extracted from quantum dynamics

A different proposal is to simulate a many-body environment quantumly once and learn a compact classical description of how it affects a small controlled subsystem. Later local-control questions could use the description without repeating the full simulation.

Luchnikov, Sonner, and Abanin [6] already study this mechanism through influence-matrix tomography. It is not new at the slogan level. Their paper also contains a generic hardness construction even for low temporal entanglement; that construction should not be rediscovered and presented as an independent useful physical advantage.

What remains demanding is a concrete physically justified regime with affordable initial-state preparation, reconstruction cost and stability, valid prediction for the stated later controls, and a strong classical comparison. Low representation dimension is not by itself an efficient-learning or useful-advantage theorem. No such regime was established in this pass.

## 6. Decision for the next exploration

Carry forward the simple organizing question: can the quantum stage produce a reusable classical replacement for a whole family of computations, rather than find one answer faster?

Graph sparsification is the clearest checked positive template, but already prior work with explicit memory/access qualifications. Keep operator compression as a mechanism to investigate, not a commitment to kernel graphs, a particular application, or another large compiler project. The first decisive task is to find an independently specified operator where generating the compressed object still favors quantum computation after access costs and strong classical structure are accounted for.

Do not build a new exhaustive search or gate compiler merely to report activity. Do not revive the manuscript, claim a novel quantum mechanism, or treat small-output size as a small-hardware bound. No new computational experiments, external contacts, paid resources, or publication submission were performed or authorized by this note.
