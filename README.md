# Quantum-Assisted Algorithm Discovery

**Can circuit-model quantum computation produce information that makes a useful
computation materially more effective than strong classical alternatives?**

Direct samples may themselves be useful outputs; they need not become a classical
program. Models and possible quantum integration come before software, datasets
and hardware implementation. Essential input and accuracy costs remain explicit.
See [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md).

**Status: exploratory. No useful quantum advantage is established.
Manuscript preparation remains on hold.**

## Current result: bound the spectral information lost by classical compression

[Spectral boundary 16](exploration/phase_3/SPECTRAL_BOUNDARY_16.md) studies the
observable space generated by an interacting spin Hamiltonian. A small tridiagonal
Lanczos model supplies a classical spectral sampler. A linewidth-weighted boundary
quantity and one residual norm bound the TV error of that sampler without knowing
the discarded spectrum. The proof specializes established recursion/resolvent
error methods; it is not a new generic Lanczos algorithm or a quantum advantage.

The structural parameter is how many observable modes must be retained at the
required linewidth, not the total Hilbert-space dimension. For nearest-neighbor
chains, an explicit local-Pauli construction uses O(n poly(m)4^m) arithmetic for
m recursion steps. Small required m therefore supplies a serious classical
alternative. Large m does not prove hardness against tensor networks or other
representations. All collective cross terms are retained; averaging independent
single-spin spectra is not generally the same task.

The [preceding model comparison](exploration/phase_3/SPECTRAL_MODEL_STRUCTURE_15.md)
provides the other side: a geometric-clock quantum sampler matched to Lorentzian
broadening, with controlled evolution time O(log(1/epsilon)/gamma). State and clock
preparation, coefficient access, simulation accuracy, readout and repeated samples
still count. The quantum route need not learn the classical recursion coefficients.
Existing direct spectral algorithms are attributed, not claimed as our invention.

The [current work order](work_orders/CURRENT.md) asks how response-relevant operator
transport scales for an alternating-offset nearest-neighbor family, and whether
symmetry, exchange narrowing, secular or tensor-network reductions remain adequate.
No molecule selection, data download, native package or large simulation is required
to begin this mathematical comparison. A sufficient bound failing is not a
quantum-opportunity certificate. No third classical spin-off is being created.

## Executed mathematical checks

```sh
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python experiments/spectral_boundary_v1/verify.py
```

Python 3.10+ and NumPy. The [report](experiments/spectral_boundary_v1/REPORT.json)
records fixed 2-, 4- and 6-spin mathematical controls, not experimental spectra,
quantum hardware or timing comparisons. The checker ran twice with identical
output; -O/-OO was rejected. The continuous-TV result follows from the written
proof, not finite bins. Floating-point tests are not interval certificates.
No earlier verifier was rerun, and earlier scientific sources/reports are unchanged.
No upstream implementation or experimental data was imported.
